# Feedback: Chapter 26 — ML-UMR

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `chapters/part5/26-ml-umr.qmd`

## Overall impression

I almost gave up in the first three paragraphs. The chapter is clearly beautifully organized and mathematically precise, but as a health researcher with weak math I was thrown into a deep pool of notation and prior-chapter references before I understood, in plain terms, what ML-UMR *is* and *does* for a single trial pair I might actually face. The biggest barrier was the density of forward and backward cross-references: by paragraph four of the Introduction I had been pointed at Bucher (Ch 14), MAIC (Ch 18), STC (Ch 19), ML-NMR (Ch 20 to 25), SEMA, SPFA, conditional constancy of absolute effects, Sobol' QMC, Gaussian copula, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, with no soft landing. The worked example at the end finally made it click, but it came after 1100 lines of formal theorems; had it appeared earlier, half my confusion would have dissolved.

## What was unclear

- The estimand in @eq-mlumr-estimand was my first hard stop. I see $\Delta_{AB}(\mathcal{P}^{*})=g(\bar\theta_A(\mathcal{P}^{*}))-g(\bar\theta_B(\mathcal{P}^{*}))$ and $\bar\theta_k=\int g^{-1}(\eta_k(\mathbf{x}))f_{*}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}$, and I had to read it three times to realize "the link scale $g$ is applied *after* averaging the response-scale outcome, not before." A one-line gloss in words ("average each arm's probability over the target population, then take the logit of each average and subtract") would have saved me. As written, the $g$ on the outside versus $g^{-1}$ on the inside is easy to misread.
- I got lost at @lem-mlumr-single-arm-conflation (lines 116 to 134). The proof says "the map $(\mu_j,\gamma_k)\mapsto\eta(\cdot)$ depends on $(\mu_j,\gamma_k)$ only through the sum $\mu_j+\gamma_k$." I understand the algebra once I stare at it, but the *concept* of "not separately identified" was not explained. I am a health researcher; "identified" is jargon. I needed one sentence like: "you cannot tell, from a single-arm trial, how much of the observed level is the treatment doing something versus just the baseline risk of these patients, because you never saw these patients without the treatment." Without that, the lemma reads as symbol manipulation.
- The decomposition $\boldsymbol\beta_k=\boldsymbol\beta_1+\boldsymbol\beta_{2,k}$ in @eq-mlumr-two-arm (lines 141 to 152) assumes I remember @def-individual-model from Chapter 20. I do not have it memorized. What is $\boldsymbol\beta_1$ again (prognostic slope, shared by all arms) versus $\boldsymbol\beta_{2,k}$ (treatment-by-covariate interaction, arm-specific)? This is load-bearing for the entire SPFA story, and it is reintroduced only by reference. I needed a two-line restatement in place.
- In @thm-mlumr-model, hypothesis (iii) "the observed comparator outcome mean lies in the open range of $g^{-1}$" confused me. The range of $g^{-1}$ for the logit is $(0,1)$, so this is saying "the observed proportion is strictly between 0 and 1." Why not say that? I spent a minute worrying about whether $g^{-1}$ here means the inverse link or something else, and whether "range" meant image or domain.
- @thm-spfa-relaxation (lines 356 to 411) lost me at the Jacobian argument. The matrix $M$ with rows $\mathbf{m}_z^\top=\int w_z(\mathbf{x})(1,\mathbf{x}^\top)f_B^{z}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}$ is presented, and then "by the constant-rank form of the inverse function theorem gives a neighborhood." I have not used the inverse function theorem since undergrad. I do not know what "constant-rank form" is, and the remark does not restate it. This proof is too terse for me to follow; I take the result on faith.
- Part (e) of @thm-mlumr-tte (the RMST scale-misalignment proof, lines 602 to 621) is the densest block in the chapter. The argument that $\Delta(\mathbf{x})$ is non-constant because "as $r(\mathbf{x})\downarrow 0$ both exponentials tend to 1 and $\Delta\to 0$, while at a finite $r(\mathbf{x})$ the two areas differ" is clever but I had to draw it to believe it. A small plot or a concrete two-$x$-value numerical check in the remark would have made it click.
- The Bayesian remark after @thm-mlumr-model (lines 335 to 344) says "one does not separate the two steps" in practice, then describes HMC. I do not know what Hamiltonian Monte Carlo is beyond the name, and the link to @def-hmc is the only help. A sentence on *why* the joint model is better than the two-step solve (uncertainty propagation across the IPD and AgD blocks) would help me, since the two-step version is the only one I actually understand from the proof.

## What wasn't elaborated enough

- The transition from @lem-mlumr-single-arm-conflation ("single arm conflates baseline and treatment") to @def-spfa ("assume the slopes are equal") is the whole logical arc of the chapter, and it is handled in two sentences around lines 136 to 153. I needed a slower bridge: "the conflation means the index arm gives us $(\alpha_A,\boldsymbol\beta_A)$, the comparator arm gives us almost nothing about its own slope, so the only way to connect them is to *assume* the comparator slope equals the index slope." The text says this, but spread across three paragraphs; a tighter narrative paragraph would have carried me.
- @thm-mlumr-target-survival (lines 800 to 833) lists six estimands in a table but does not explain *when a health researcher wants which one*. I know RMST is clinically interpretable, but why would I pick marginal survival versus median survival versus marginal hazard ratio? The remark afterward says "report the conditional log HR as primary," but the table itself is unmotivated.
- The "Type 1" versus "Type 2" terminology appears in remarks (lines 329, 424 to 433) but is never given a definition box. I pieced together that Type 1 = SPFA + overall AgD and Type 2 = relaxed + subgroup AgD, but I am not sure, and the terms recur as if settled. A short definition or a margin note would fix this.
- The Gaussian copula reconstruction of $f_B$ from margins and a correlation matrix (referenced in @thm-mlumr-qmc-marginalization, lines 445 to 467) is dispatched in one theorem that leans entirely on Chapter 22. For the unanchored setting, the *quality* of this reconstruction matters a lot (the limits remark at line 1093 says misspecification biases $\alpha_B$), but I never saw an example of what "reported margins and a correlation matrix" looks like in a real trial report, or how often a correlation matrix is actually published (rarely, in my experience). This gap between theory and HTA reality is not confronted.
- The identifiability frontier @thm-mlumr-identifiability (lines 1041 to 1059) is the summary theorem, but cases (1) to (4) are stated very tersely. Case (2), "over-identified, the remaining $Z-1$ equations are testable constraints," deserved one more sentence on *how* one tests them (a posterior predictive check? a likelihood ratio?), because as a researcher this is the one practically actionable idea in the limits section and it is gone in a clause.

## Missing motivation / "why does this matter?"

- The Introduction tells me unanchored disconnected networks are "the daily reality of HTA for single-arm oncology trials, rare-disease registries, and real-world cohorts" (lines 23 to 26). That is the strongest motivating sentence in the chapter, and it is excellent, but it is the *only* concrete HTA scenario. I wanted a named example: a single-arm pembrolizumab trial versus a real-world cohort on chemotherapy, or a CAR-T registry versus an external control arm. The chapter never names a drug, a disease, or a trial. For a method whose whole point is HTA applicability, this is a conspicuous absence.
- WHY prove @prp-spfa-vs-sema so formally? As a researcher I "feel" that unanchored needs a stronger assumption, but the four-part proposition with a strictness proof is more than I expected. The motivation in the remark at lines 1022 to 1031 helps ("one picture"), but it comes *after* the proof. Lead with the picture.
- WHY is the equivalence with unanchored STC (@thm-mlumr-stc-equivalence) worth a full theorem and proof? The honest remark at lines 942 to 948 answers this ("value added is not at the comparator but at other targets"), but again it follows the formal result. I would have liked the motivation first: "we want to know exactly what ML-UMR buys over the simpler method you already know, so we prove they agree in the comparator population and differ elsewhere."
- The survival section (@sec-mlumr-tte) opens by saying survival "motivates ML-UMR in oncology HTA" (line 511) but never returns to a concrete oncology example in the formal development. The coda in the worked example (lines 1228 to 1243) is the only concrete survival scenario, and it uses abstract $\lambda$ values, not a named cancer or a realistic hazard. I wanted "this is the typical situation in single-arm melanoma trials where the comparator is a published chemotherapy KM curve."
- The whole question of *when a researcher should prefer ML-UMR over ML-NMR* is not directly addressed. The Introduction says ML-NMR needs connectivity (the bridge), and ML-UMR removes it. But in practice, if I have even one connecting study, should I use ML-NMR instead? The "When to use ML-UMR" remark (lines 1119 to 1127) lists conditions but does not contrast them with "when to use ML-NMR instead." A small decision table would help.

## Missing examples and intuition

- The worked example (@sec-mlumr-example, lines 1132 to 1243) is genuinely excellent and was the point where the chapter clicked for me. But it appears after 1100 lines. Consider moving a *skeleton* of it, or a one-arm toy version, into the Introduction or right after @def-spfa, so the reader has a concrete handle before the identifiability theorems.
- There is no intermediate example between the binary two-point sum and the full survival case. A *continuous identity-link* example would be trivial (closed form) but would show the contrast with the logit case (where aggregation is inexact) and reinforce why the comparator *distribution* matters for logit but only the *mean* matters for identity. The remark at lines 316 to 333 says this in prose; a three-line numerical example would make it tangible.
- @thm-pseudo-ipd-reconstruction (reconstructing pseudo-IPD from a Kaplan-Meier curve) is a beautifully concrete theorem, and it is one of the few places where I felt on solid ground because the KM estimator was restated (@eq-km-product, lines 658 to 665). But there is no worked numerical illustration of the inversion @eq-km-inversion. A tiny 3-event toy KM curve with explicit $d_j,n_j$ numbers would make the inversion formula immediately legible.
- The subgroup relaxation (@thm-spfa-relaxation) has a nice remark about "two binary effect modifiers crossed into four subgroups" (lines 417 to 421), but no concrete example of *what a subgroup report looks like in a published paper*. Do trial reports actually give event rates stratified by age and sex? In my experience this is rare, and the remark at lines 424 to 433 confirms it, but a one-sentence real-world grounding ("this is why the relaxed model is rarely usable in practice") would land harder.
- There is no diagram anywhere. A single figure showing (a) the disconnected two-node network, (b) the index and comparator covariate distributions as two overlapping densities, and (c) the SPFA projection of the index surface onto the comparator arm with a level shift, would replace several paragraphs of explanation. The text is otherwise figure-free, which for a geometric method is a loss.

## Notation and jargon problems

- "Identified" / "identification" / "identifiability" is used throughout (lines 116, 252, 276, 356, 1041) without a definition box or a gloss. For a beginner this is core jargon. Either a margin note or a forward reference to a definition in Part I would help.
- The symbol $\theta_{\bullet B}$ (lines 266, 280, 1086) is not in `notation.qmd`. I guessed it means "the aggregate comparator mean" from context, but the bullet subscript is unexplained. The notation file has $\theta_{ijk}$ for conditional means and $d_{ab(\mathcal{P})}$ for marginal effects, but no $\theta_{\bullet k}$. This should be added or the symbol explained on first use.
- $\pi_{\mathrm{Ind}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{Agg}}$ (lines 260, 268, 748) are used for the individual and aggregate likelihood kernels but are not in `notation.qmd` either. I inferred their meaning, but a beginner might not.
- The phrase "conflated intercept" (lines 140, 147) is the author's coinage and is intuitively fine, but it recurs as a term of art without a formal definition; the lemma defines the conflation but the *noun* "conflated intercept" is then used freely. A one-line definition in @def-unanchored-network or @lem-mlumr-single-arm-conflation would pin it.
- "$\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{PF}}$" with the subscript $\mathrm{PF}$ for "prognostic factor" (lines 178, 187, 255) is clear once decoded, but the first use (line 178) does not spell out what PF stands for. I back-solved it from "shared prognostic factor assumption." Spell it out on first use.
- "Hardy-Krause variation" $V_{\mathrm{HK}}$ (line 461) and "star discrepancy" $D_N^{*}$ (line 463) are in `notation.qmd` (lines 144 to 145), good, but they appear in the theorem statement without any intuition. A beginner has no sense of what bounded HK variation *means*; even "intuitively, the function is not too wiggly" would help. This is a QMC chapter dependency, but a one-clause gloss is cheap.
- "Frailty" (line 596) appears in the proof of @thm-mlumr-tte(d) without definition. In survival analysis frailty is a standard term, but a beginner health researcher may not know it means a multiplicative random effect on the hazard, here $\rho(\mathbf{x})=e^{\mathbf{x}^\top\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{PF}}}$.

## Pacing issues

- The Introduction (lines 5 to 75) is very long and very dense. It introduces the setting, SPFA, SEMA, conditional constancy, the Sobol'/copula machinery, the chapter roadmap, and the notation conventions all in one breath. I would split it: keep the narrative motivation (lines 5 to 48), then a short "Chapter map" paragraph, then push the notation conventions (lines 68 to 75) into the setting section where the symbols first appear.
- There is a long stretch of formal theorems from @thm-mlumr-model (line 252) through @thm-mlumr-target-survival (line 800) with no concrete example or rest stop. That is roughly 550 lines of proofs and remarks before the worked example. A reader like me drowns here. Inserting the skeleton of the binary example after @thm-mlumr-model, and a concrete survival scenario after @thm-mlumr-tte, would break the formal stretch into digestible chunks.
- The QMC section (@sec-mlumr-qmc, lines 437 to 508) is short and almost entirely a corollary of Chapter 22. That is fine, but it sits between the relaxed-model theorem and the survival section, and the pacing drops because the reader is sent back to Chapter 22 for everything. A two-paragraph self-contained summary of "what the pipeline does, concretely" before the theorem would let this section stand on its own.
- The SPFA-versus-SEMA section (@sec-mlumr-spfa-sema, lines 953 to 1034) comes *after* the STC equivalence section, but logically it underpins both. I would move it earlier, right after @def-spfa and @lem-spfa-absolute-constancy, so the reader already knows SPFA is strictly stronger than SEMA before encountering the identifiability and equivalence theorems that depend on that fact.
- The Notes section (lines 1245 to 1297) is extremely thorough but very long for a single chapter; as a beginner I found it useful as a reference but skipped it on first read. That is probably fine, but the "no machine-checked counterpart" paragraph (lines 1284 to 1297) is interesting and might earn a margin note earlier rather than being buried.

## What worked well

- The worked binary example (@sec-mlumr-example) is the gold standard for what I needed: every number is checkable with a calculator, the steps are labeled and cross-referenced to the theorems they instantiate, and Step 6 (the SPFA-violation robustness/fragility contrast) made the entire identifiability frontier suddenly concrete. This is the section that taught me the chapter.
- The "honest summary" remarks (e.g., lines 942 to 948, 1085 to 1097, 1119 to 1127) are genuinely helpful. The author is candid about the limits (untestable assumption, no unmeasured-confounding fix, no heterogeneity term, fragile extrapolation), and this honesty builds trust. The remark at lines 1085 to 1097 on inherent limits is the best single paragraph in the chapter for a researcher.
- The narrative framing of "every method until now has needed a bridge" (lines 7 to 19) and "this chapter removes that bridge" (line 21) is a vivid, memorable way to locate ML-UMR in the book's arc. Even when I was lost in the math, I always knew *where* I was methodologically. The bridge metaphor is excellent pedagogy.
- The survival coda in the worked example (lines 1228 to 1243) with explicit marginal HR values at $t=0,5,10,50$ made the abstract "marginal HR is time-varying" claim (@thm-mlumr-tte(d)) instantly legible. More such mini-numerical illustrations throughout would transform the chapter.

6. Spell out $\theta_{\bullet B}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{Ind}}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{Agg}}$, and $\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{PF}}$ on first use and add the first two to `notation.qmd`.
7. Rewrite hypothesis (iii) of @thm-mlumr-model in plain terms for the logit example ("the observed proportion is strictly between 0 and 1").
8. Lead the SPFA-vs-SEMA section with the "one picture" remark (lines 1022 to 1031) *before* the formal proposition, then prove.
9. Add one figure: the disconnected network, the two covariate densities, and the SPFA level-shift projection.
10. Add a small decision table contrasting ML-UMR, ML-NMR, unanchored MAIC, and unanchored STC on (assumption, target populations, data needs).

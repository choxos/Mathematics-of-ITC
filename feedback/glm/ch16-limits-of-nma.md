# Feedback: Chapter 16 — Limits of NMA

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `chapters/part3/16-limits-of-nma.qmd`

## Overall impression

I came into this chapter expecting to understand *when network meta-analysis gives me wrong answers*, and in principle I leave with that understanding, but only after a hard fight. The chapter has two big ideas I genuinely need: (1) Bucher's indirect comparison is biased when an effect modifier is imbalanced across trials, and (2) trying to fix it with trial-level meta-regression fails because of ecological bias. These are exactly the questions that keep me up at night when I read an NMA report. The trouble is that the chapter front-loads an enormous amount of notation and conceptual scaffolding (marginal vs average-conditional contrasts, two flavors of constancy, collapsibility on scales) before I have a single concrete scenario in my head, and by the time I reach the actual failure theorem I have lost the thread. The worked examples at the end are excellent and probably should have come much earlier; I understood the bias decomposition in five minutes of reading `exm-limits-bucher-rd` that I had not understood in twenty minutes of reading `sec-limits-failure-bucher`. The chapter is clearly rigorous and clearly important, but it reads like it was written for someone who already finished Parts I and II last week, not for a researcher coming up fresh.

## What was unclear

- **`sec-limits-intro` (lines 5-53)** packs the entire conceptual roadmap, the two failures, the two missing ingredients, and the forward pointer to Part IV into one intro. As a noob I cannot absorb "constancy of relative effects, equivalently transitivity, the assumption that the relative effect of one treatment versus another is the same number in every study population" (lines 11-13) without a concrete drug example. Which trial? Which population? What does "same number" mean when the outcome is binary?
- **`def-conditional-marginal-contrast` (lines 62-77)**: the two definitions are stated back to back with almost identical symbols (`d_{ab(\mathcal P)}` vs `\tilde d_{ab(\mathcal P)}`). The tilde is the only distinguishing mark and I kept losing track of which is which. The sentence "The first marginalizes inside the link, the second outside" (line 76) is the whole intuition and it is given one line; I needed a diagram or a labelled picture of where `g` sits relative to the integral.
- **`prp-limits-collapse-link` (lines 86-98)** and its proof (lines 100-113): the proof of (2) just says "this is the definition of direct collapsibility" and points at `@eq-emcoll-direct`. If I do not have that equation memorized, the proof is a citation, not a proof. For a noob this is a step skipped.
- **`lem-limits-em-breaks-transitivity` (lines 158-182)** part (2): the construction "take `f_1,f_2` to put all their mass near `x_1` and near `x_2`" is the heart of the argument but it is waved at. Why is it legitimate to choose point masses when earlier we assumed densities? The parenthetical "point masses `\delta_{x_1},\delta_{x_2}` if the support is discrete, narrow densities otherwise" (lines 178-179) hides a limit argument I would have liked spelled out.
- **`thm-failure-of-bucher` (lines 207-232)**: the bias expression @eq-limits-bias is the centerpiece, but the proof (lines 234-265) does the algebra by saying "linearity of the integral gives" and then a chain of equalities. I could follow it mechanically but I never got the *physical* picture: which term is "the trial over-representing the high-risk stratum"? A sentence mapping `b_{AC}>0` to "trial AC sampled too many `x=1` patients relative to target" would have made it click.
- **The proof of (1) "vanishing under no modification" (lines 252-260)** uses the converse construction again without connecting it to intuition. "Choosing `f_{tC}` concentrated near `x_2` and `f_*` near `x_1`" is exactly the imbalance story but it is presented as a measure-theoretic trick.
- **`lem-limits-aggregation-jensen` (lines 358-381)**: the proof leans entirely on Jensen applied to `\eta_t(X)` as the random variable. I had to reread to notice the variable inside `g^{-1}` is `\eta_t(X)`, not `X`. This substitution is load-bearing and not flagged; a noob will apply Jensen to `X` and get confused.
- **`lem-limits-probit-integral` (lines 417-443)**: the trick "let `U\sim\mathcal N(0,1)` independent of `Z`, then `\Phi(a+bZ)=P(U\le a+bZ\mid Z)`" is clever but presented as if obvious. I have basic calculus; the probabilists' identity `\Phi(t)=P(U\le t)` needs to be stated explicitly the first time it is used, because for me `\Phi` is just a table of numbers.
- **`thm-ecological-norecovery` proof (lines 470-503)**: the differentiation `h'(\rho)=(1+\rho^2\sigma^2)^{-3/2}` (lines 489-492) is correct but the chain-rule / quotient-rule steps are collapsed into two displayed lines. I lost the numerator simplification `(1+\rho^2\sigma^2)-\rho^2\sigma^2=1`. One intermediate line would have saved me ten minutes.
- **Lines 487-503** ("fundamental theorem of calculus ... the integrand is positive ... strictly below 1 except at one point"): the logical move "strictly below 1 except at one point, therefore the integral is strictly below the integral of 1" is true but I had to think about whether a single point of equality breaks the strict inequality. It does not, but a one-sentence justification ("a single point has Lebesgue measure zero and does not affect the integral") would help the calculus-weak reader.
- **`cor-limits-pv-no-bias` proof (lines 286-298)**: the integral `\int\tau^g_{tC}(x_{EM})[\int(f_{tC}-f_*)(x_{EM},w)dw]dx_{EM}` (lines 294-295) uses Fubini to integrate out `W` first. For me this is advanced; the chapter does not say "we swap the order of integration," it just does it. The phrase "integrating out `W` first" (line 292) is the only signal.

## What wasn't elaborated enough

- **Why "anchored" comparisons can ignore prognostic variables** (`cor-limits-pv-no-bias`, lines 277-306). This is the most practically important result in the chapter for an HTA analyst: it tells me I only need to balance effect modifiers, not every baseline covariate. The proof is correct but the *reason* deserves a paragraph of plain English: in a contrast, any covariate effect that enters both arms equally cancels. I got that from the slogan on line 301, not from the proof.
- **The "non-collapsibility surcharge" remark (lines 331-341)** is the bridge between the two halves of the chapter and it gets one paragraph. The claim that on the log odds ratio scale the bias is "@eq-limits-bias *plus* a Jensen gap" is exactly the kind of statement a health researcher meets constantly (everyone knows ORs are "weird") and it deserved its own subsection with a small numeric illustration. Instead it defers to "the next section," but the next section is about ecological bias, not about compounding.
- **The two "constancies" of `sec-limits-two-constancies` (lines 141-156)**: the distinction between marginal constancy and conditional constancy is the conceptual hinge of the whole chapter and it is introduced in four paragraphs. I needed an explicit contrast table: "Marginal constancy says X; conditional constancy says Y; Bucher needs X; conditional constancy can hold while X fails, and the gap is Z." The text says all this but spread across prose.
- **`def-ecological-bias` (lines 388-408)** defines arm-level ecological bias and then *coefficient*-level ecological bias in the same definition block, with the latter described in words rather than a display equation. The coefficient-level one is the one that matters for ITCs and it is relegated to prose (lines 403-407). I would split it into its own equation.
- **The relationship between ecological bias and non-collapsibility** (remark lines 521-535) is described as "the same Jensen gap." This unification is beautiful and is arguably the chapter's main theoretical payoff, but it is buried in a remark. It deserves a labeled proposition with a short proof, because right now it reads as a passing observation.

## Missing motivation / "why does this matter?"

- **No clinical scenario anywhere in the chapter.** Treatments are `A,B,C`; the covariate is `X`; the outcome is "a binary event (an unfavorable outcome, so larger risk is worse)" (line 547). I am a health researcher: I want one named disease, one named drug class, one named covariate (age? baseline severity? HER2 status?). Without it the math floats free. The Bucher example (lines 545-614) would be transformed by naming A/B/C as, say, two biologics versus standard of care for plaque psoriasis, with X being prior biologic exposure. This is not decoration; it is how I map the formula onto my own systematic review.
- **Why NMA fails *matters* for health technology assessment is never said explicitly.** Lines 17-25 say the bias "can be large enough to reverse the sign of the estimated treatment difference," which is the headline, but the HTA stakes (a reimbursement decision going the wrong way, patients denied an effective drug, or paying for an ineffective one) are absent. A sentence connecting "sign reversal" to "wrong recommendation" to "NICE TSD 18" would tie the math to my job.
- **The remark "Where the standard error is silent" (lines 308-316)** is the single most important practical point for a noob: a tight confidence interval does not protect you. But it is a remark, not a highlighted callout. This deserves a box or a "Warning" environment. Every junior analyst I know has trusted a narrow CI on an indirect comparison; this is the paragraph that disabuses them, and it should shout.
- **The remark "What is and is not testable" (lines 318-329)** is similarly crucial and similarly subdued. The statement "in the minimal anchored triangle ... @eq-limits-bias is then untestable from the trial outcomes" (lines 322-323) is the reason this chapter is not a curiosity but a *threat*: you cannot detect the bias from the data you have. That deserves prominence.
- **`lem-limits-conditional-additivity` (lines 121-139)** is proved but never motivated: why do I care that `\tau_{AB}(x)=\tau_{AC}(x)-\tau_{BC}(x)` pointwise? The answer (because it is what lets Bucher subtract *at all*, and because the bias is precisely the failure of that subtraction to survive integration against different densities) is implicit. Say it.

## Missing examples and intuition

- **The chapter has exactly two worked examples (lines 545-667)** and both are good, but they arrive only after all the theory. A small running example threaded through `sec-limits-setup` and `sec-limits-failure-bucher`, with two hypothetical trials and a binary covariate, would have carried me through the abstract definitions. The eventual `exm-limits-bucher-rd` could literally be that thread, moved up.
- **No figure.** A picture of two trial populations as overlapping covariate histograms, with the conditional contrast `\tau(x)` drawn as a curve over them, and the bias shaded as the mismatch, would convey `@eq-limits-bias` instantly. The chapter is pure prose and equations.
- **No example of the non-collapsibility surcharge** within the chapter body. Exercise `exr-limits-logit-compound` (lines 763-771) asks the reader to compute it, but a noob wants the solution in the text, not in the problem set.
- **No numeric example of ecological bias** before `exm-limits-probit-eco` (lines 616-667). The probit integral `@eq-limits-probit-integral` is derived in the abstract; a one-line numeric check (e.g., "`\mathbb E[\Phi(0+1\cdot X)]` vs `\Phi(0)` for `X\sim\mathcal N(0,1)`") before the full theorem would ground it.
- **No example contrasting a prognostic variable vs an effect modifier** in concrete terms, even though the distinction is the chapter's most reused concept. A two-row table ("Age shifts both arms equally -> prognostic -> cancels; biomarker shifts the treatment benefit -> effect modifier -> biases") would be worth a thousand words of `cor-limits-pv-no-bias`.

## Notation and jargon problems

- **`d_{ab(\mathcal P)}` vs `\tilde d_{ab(\mathcal P)}`** (defined lines 62-76): the tilde is too slight a marker for the central distinction of the chapter. Consider `d^{\text{marg}}` and `d^{\text{cond}}`, or named them "marginal" and "average-conditional" with distinct letter families.
- **`\Delta^{\mathrm{Cond},g}_{AB}(\mathcal P^{*})`** (line 200) introduces yet a third symbol for essentially the average-conditional contrast in a *target* population. How does it relate to `\tilde d_{AB(\mathcal P^*)}`? The notation table in `notation.qmd` lists `d_{ab(\mathcal P)}` and `\hat d_{ab(j)}` but not `\tilde d` nor `\Delta^{\mathrm{Cond},g}`. These new symbols should either be added to `notation.qmd` or defined locally with a pointer that they are synonyms.
- **"anchored triangle"** (line 194) and "anchored comparison" (line 184, 302, 304) are used repeatedly but never defined in this chapter; they presumably come from Chapter 14. A one-line reminder in a margin note would save the reader a back-flip.
- **"directly collapsible"** (lines 93, 108, 115, 162) vs "collapsible" vs "non-collapsible": the qualifier "directly" is doing work I do not fully grasp. `def-collapsibility` is referenced but the adjective "directly" is not explained here. Is there an *indirectly* collapsible scale? If so, is it relevant? If not, drop the qualifier.
- **"conditional additivity"** (line 119) and "pointwise additivity" (line 122) are used as if synonymous; the lemma title says "Pointwise additivity of conditional contrasts" but the body calls it "conditional additivity." Pick one.
- **"shared effect modifier assumption (SEMA)"** appears in `notation.qmd` (line 146) but not in this chapter, even though "conditional constancy" (line 149) looks like exactly SEMA. If they are the same, say so; if not, distinguish. The bridge from the book's term to the TSD literature term is missing.
- **`b_{AC}, b_{BC}`** in `@eq-limits-bias` (lines 220-221) are named with a lowercase `b` that collides visually with the treatment letter `B`. In `exm-limits-bucher-rd` I had to reread to be sure `b_{AC}` was a bias term and not "B versus C."
- **"trial reports"** (line 79) is used loosely: a two-arm randomized trial "reports" a marginal effect, but in practice a trial reports arm-level event counts and the analyst computes the marginal contrast. The shorthand hides the estimation step a noob needs to see.
- **"inverse link" / "`g^{-1}`"** (lines 355, 386): the chapter switches between calling `g^{-1}` the "inverse link" and just writing it. A reminder that for logit the inverse link is the logistic function and for probit it is `\Phi` would orient readers who met these in Chapter 7 but not recently.
- **"affine in `x`"** (line 360, 390): "affine" is jargon for "linear plus a constant." Stating that once would help.

## Pacing issues

- **`sec-limits-setup` (lines 55-119)** introduces marginal vs average-conditional contrasts, collapsibility, pointwise additivity, and the two constancies all before any failure is shown. This is roughly 60 lines of pure definitional scaffolding with no motivating example. For a noob this is the wall. Inserting a concrete two-trial numerical thread here would fix the pacing.
- **`sec-limits-two-constancies` (lines 141-189)** is conceptually heavy and precedes the main theorem. It could be shortened: the lemma `lem-limits-em-breaks-transitivity` is essentially a warm-up for `thm-failure-of-bucher` and somewhat duplicates its clauses (1) and (2). Consider merging or trimming.
- **`sec-limits-ecological` (lines 343-535)** is the longest section and covers: the Jensen gap, the definition of ecological bias, the Gaussian probit integral, the full attenuation theorem, and two remarks. The probit integral lemma is a digression into a calculation; it interrupts the argumentative flow. It might sit better as a sidebar or be moved just ahead of the theorem that uses it, with a clearer "we now need a computational lemma" signpost.
- **The remarks (lines 308-341, 511-535)** are dense and numerous; six remarks across two sections. They are individually valuable but collectively dilute the main narrative. Some are nearly theorem-grade (the non-collapsibility compounding, the equivalence of ecological bias and non-collapsibility) and should be promoted.
- **`sec-limits-gap` (lines 669-708)** returns to the "two missing ingredients" framing from the intro and effectively re-states the chapter's motivation. This is the third time I have been told about the two ingredients (intro lines 39-45, ecological remark lines 511-519, and here). Consolidating would tighten the chapter by a page.
- **The worked examples (lines 537-667)** come after the entire theory and after the "gap" section already gestures at the cure. They would serve better interleaved, with `exm-limits-bucher-rd` right after `thm-failure-of-bucher` and `exm-limits-probit-eco` right after `thm-ecological-norecovery`.

## What worked well

- **The two worked examples (lines 545-667) are excellent.** Concrete numbers, every step shown, the sign-reversal in `exm-limits-bucher-rd` is genuinely striking, and the "verification through @eq-limits-bias" block (lines 599-613) is a model for how to connect a theorem to a computation. The probit example's "two trials, checked directly" sub-block (lines 643-653) actually reconstructing `b_2` from two points was the moment the ecological-bias theorem finally landed for me.
- **The "cure" paragraph of `exm-limits-bucher-rd` (lines 611-613)** makes the bridge to population adjustment tangible in two lines of arithmetic. This is the clearest sentence in the chapter.
- **The closing section `sec-limits-gap` (lines 669-708)** is a strong synthesis: it names the two ingredients, maps each of MAIC/STC/ML-NMR to them, and explicitly says ML-NMR "confronts the aggregation gap head on by computing it instead of ignoring it" (line 702). That last phrase is memorable and accurate.
- **The exercises (lines 742-787)** are well designed: `exr-limits-zero-bias-trap` (the "trap" of an effect modifier that happens to give zero bias) directly tests whether I understood the *iff* in clause (1), and `exr-limits-jensen-sign` connects to the lognormal mean, which I actually remember from somewhere. They are pitched at the right level for a determined beginner.
- **The remark "Where the standard error is silent" (lines 308-316)** is, content-wise, the most important paragraph in the chapter. Even though I argued it should be more prominent, the substance is exactly right and clearly stated.
- **The machine-checked tag on `cor-limits-pv-no-bias` (line 283)** is well placed and the proof's reference to the Coq lemma name `anchored_PV_cancellation` (line 290) is specific. This is the chapter's one tagged result and it earns the tag.
- **The honest acknowledgment in the notes (lines 726-736)** that the probit is "the one case where the aggregation integral is elementary" is refreshing; it tells me the general case (Chapter 20) is genuinely harder and this is the toy version.

## Suggestions

1. **Open with a clinical scenario.** One paragraph naming a disease, three drugs, one binary effect modifier (e.g., prior treatment exposure), and two trials with different modifier prevalences. Carry it as a thread through every section, not just the worked example at the end.
2. **Add a figure** showing two trial covariate distributions, the conditional contrast curve, and the shaded bias regions corresponding to `b_{AC}` and `b_{BC}`. One good diagram teaches `@eq-limits-bias` faster than the proof.
3. **Promote the two key remarks to highlighted callouts** ("Warning: the standard error cannot see this bias"; "Warning: in the anchored triangle this bias is untestable"). These are the messages a health researcher must not miss.
4. **Interleave the worked examples** with the theorems: place `exm-limits-bucher-rd` immediately after `thm-failure-of-bucher`, and `exm-limits-probit-eco` immediately after `thm-ecological-norecovery`.
5. **Disambiguate the two `d` symbols.** Either rename (`d^{\text{marg}}`, `d^{\text{ac}}`) or add an explicit reminder box at the top of each section stating which is which. Add `\tilde d` and `\Delta^{\mathrm{Cond},g}` to `notation.qmd`.
6. **Expand the "non-collapsibility surcharge" remark (lines 331-341) into a short subsection** with a numeric OR-scale illustration, since this is the compounding that health researchers routinely misunderstand.
7. **Add a one-row table distinguishing prognostic variable vs effect modifier** with a concrete example, placed near `cor-limits-pv-no-bias`, and clarify whether "conditional constancy" is the same as the literature's SEMA.
8. **Spell out the Fubini and measure-zero steps** in `cor-limits-pv-no-bias` and the FTC step in `thm-ecological-norecovery` with one extra line each; the noob loses hours to these two silent moves.
9. **Soften the jargon "affine," "anchored," "directly collapsible"** with parenthetical glosses on first use in the chapter, or a short "terms carried from Chapter 14/11" box at the top.
10. **Consider a short "what a valid indirect comparison must compute" boxed summary** right after `sec-limits-gap`, listing the two ingredients as a checklist. This is the takeaway Part IV builds on and it should be impossible to miss.

# Feedback: Appendix D — Special Functions

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `appendices/D-special-functions.qmd`

## Overall impression

I came to this appendix expecting a catalog of the "special functions" the book uses (the title even says so), but what I got is a deep technical reference for four very specific objects tied to ML-NMR numerical integration (the normal MGF, the error function, Owen's T, and the radical inverse / Sobol construction) plus two probit approximations to the logistic. It is mathematically rigorous and proved in full, which I respect, but it is NOT the broad "gamma, digamma, beta, log-gamma, Bessel, hypergeometric, softmax, expit/logit" catalog the brief implied. As a noob, I finished the appendix more confused than I started on several points: functions are named without being motivated, the connection to actual ITC/health technology assessment work is thin, and the pacing assumes I already know what these functions are for. The writing is competent but speaks to an already-trained mathematician, not to someone who has never seen a moment generating function and is meeting "differentiate under the integral" for the second time in their life.

## What was unclear

- **The title lies about scope.** The YAML title (line 2) is "Special Functions for Numerical Integration," and the opening paragraph (lines 5-16) lists exactly four objects plus two approximations. There is no gamma function, no digamma, no beta function, no log-gamma, no Bessel, no hypergeometric anywhere in this file. If those appear elsewhere (Binomial/multinomial likelihoods in Chapter 21? Beta-binomial? Dirichlet?), I have no idea where to look. As a reader I feel misled by the generic appendix letter "D — Special Functions."
- **"Moment generating function" is used before it is explained.** Lines 8 and 22 reference `@def-mgf` and `@def-mvn`, but I have never internalized WHY an expectation of $e^{tX}$ is useful or what it "generates." The one sentence at line 31 ("turns the average of an exponential into an algebraic expression") is the only intuition; it is too compressed. A noob needs: "the MGF is called that because differentiating it $k$ times at $t=0$ gives the $k$-th moment $\mathbb{E}[X^k]$; we use it not for moments but because the log link makes the aggregate mean literally $\mathbb{E}[e^W]$, which is the MGF at $t=1$."
- **"Complete the square" (line 62) is asserted as a known technique.** I can follow the algebra once it is written down, but I would not have produced the step $(x-\mu)^2-2\sigma^2 t x = (x-(\mu+\sigma^2 t))^2 - 2\sigma^2 t\mu - \sigma^4 t^2$ myself. The phrase "as one verifies by expanding the right side" (line 67) is exactly the kind of handwave that loses a beginner. Show the expansion.
- **The multivariate proof (lines 79-93) leans on a representation I do not own.** It invokes `@thm-spectral-theorem` for a symmetric square root $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}$ and the affine-image construction $\mathbf{X}=\boldsymbol{\mu}+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}\mathbf{Z}$. For a weak-math reader, the jump "covariance of this construction equals $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$" needs the explicit $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ line spelled out, which is only half shown at line 92.
- **Owen's T definition (lines 280-285) is unmotivated.** I see the integral $T(h,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^a \frac{\exp(-\frac12 h^2(1+x^2))}{1+x^2}dx$ and I have no idea where the $1+x^2$ denominator or the $2\pi$ came from. The probabilistic meaning (wedge probability, line 297) comes only AFTER a lemma and a proof. A beginner needs the wedge picture FIRST, then the integral as a computation of that picture.
- **The Plackett heat equation (lines 359-390) is the hardest derivation in the appendix and gets the least intuition.** "The density's derivative in the correlation equals its mixed second derivative in the two coordinates" (lines 360-361) is stated as a fact about to be proved, but WHY is that true physically or probabilistically? Why would anyone think to differentiate in $\rho$? I had to take it on faith.
- **The differentiate-in-$\rho$ proof of @thm-owens-t-identity (lines 405-446) is very dense.** The chain of "define $a(\rho)$, claim $G$ has the same derivative and same value at $\rho=0$" is a standard trick, but for a noob the verification (lines 429-444) is a wall of algebra with no checkpoints. I lost the thread at $a'(\rho)$.
- **Radical inverse (lines 660-670): "reflecting the digits about the radix point."** I had to read this three times. A literal worked digit reflection BEFORE the formal sum would help (the example at line 790 does this, but it comes 120 lines later).
- **$(t,m,s)$-net and "star discrepancy" (lines 680-682) are used without a beginner definition here.** They point to `@def-sobol-net` and `@def-star-discrepancy` in Chapter 22, but if I am reading the appendix as a standalone reference (which appendices are for), I am stuck.

## What wasn't elaborated enough

- **The error function's PURPOSE (section @sec-sf-erf, lines 157-268).** Line 159 says "the normal distribution function has no elementary antiderivative, so numerical analysis works with a rescaled, standardized version." But I never learned WHY this particular rescaling $\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^x e^{-t^2}dt$ is the canonical one, as opposed to just $\int_0^x e^{-t^2}dt$ or $\Phi$ itself. The remark at lines 258-268 ("why software standardizes on erf") helps but arrives after two heavy proofs.
- **The series and asymptotic expansion (lines 218-256).** The Maclaurin series is derived in one paragraph; the asymptotic expansion via integration by parts is one paragraph plus a "the expansion is asymptotic rather than convergent" sentence (lines 248-251). For a noob, the distinction asymptotic-vs-convergent deserves a paragraph: what does it MEAN that the terms eventually grow, and why is it still useful?
- **Mills ratio (lines 229-231).** "so in particular $1-\Phi(x)\sim\phi(x)/x$" is dropped without explaining the tilde notation or why this matters for tail probabilities in practice (e.g., computing $p$-values or rare-event rates in HTA).
- **The two-probit moment system (lines 591-628).** The jump from the three moment conditions @eq-sf-two-probit-moments to the numerical solution @eq-sf-two-probit-coeffs is a black box: "solving it numerically yields" (line 623). I have no idea what method, whether the solution is unique, or whether the coefficients are sensitive. A noob-friendly sentence on "this is a nonlinear system; here is why it has a unique real solution" would help.
- **The expected-squared-probit proof (lines 478-500).** The conditioning trick $\Phi(a+bZ)=P(U\le a+bZ\mid Z)$ is the key idea and is stated in one clause at line 480. This is the single most beautiful idea in the appendix and it gets one sentence. Expand it: why is conditioning on $Z$ legitimate, why does it turn a square of a CDF into a bivariate probability?
- **The scale-mixture representation (lines 527-540).** "The standard logistic distribution is a scale mixture of normals: there is a density $w(s)$..." The existence of $w(s)$ is asserted, $w$ is never named or plotted, and the reader is told the construction does NOT use $w$ directly (line 531). Then why mention it? I was more confused after reading this paragraph.

## Missing motivation / "why does this matter?"

- **No chapter-by-chapter map of which function is used where.** The intro (lines 5-16) names Part V and a few slugs, but a noob wants a table: "Gamma/digamma — used in Chapter 21 for Poisson/multinomial likelihoods; error function — used in Chapter 22 for normal CDF evaluation; Owen's T — used in Chapter 20 for probit aggregation; radical inverse — used in Chapter 22 Sobol' QMC." Such a table does not exist. The Notes section (lines 803-844) partially fills this but only at the end and only for some functions.
- **WHY is the log link the one case with a closed form?** Line 34-35 says so, but I want the conceptual reason: the log link turns a mean into an exponential of a linear predictor, and the exponential is exactly what the normal MGF computes. Say it in one sentence for the slow reader.
- **WHY does probit aggregation need the SECOND moment (line 462-464)?** The text says "the probit-link aggregate likelihood needs not only the first moment... but also its second moment, which Owen's T supplies." But WHY does the likelihood need the second moment? Is it for the Bernoulli variance (which is mentioned at line 473)? For a Fisher information? For a sandwich variance? The motivation is buried. As an HTA researcher I want to know: this matters because the binomial likelihood depends on both the mean and the variance of the response.
- **WHY quasi-Monte Carlo at all (lines 651-658)?** The contrast with $N^{-1/2}$ Monte Carlo is mentioned only in the remark at line 714-715, deep in the weeds. A noob needs the motivation up front: "independent random points give $O(N^{-1/2})$ error; Sobol' points give $O((\log N)^d/N)$, exponentially better, which is why ML-NMR uses them."
- **No health/ITC domain connection anywhere.** This appendix reads as a pure math chapter. There is not a single sentence connecting these functions to a trial, a treatment effect, a cost-effectiveness calculation, or an HTA decision. The closest is "the precision a health technology assessment reports" at line 647. More of that: when would a reviewer actually encounter Owen's T? When fitting a probit-link NMA to binary responder data.
- **Missing: where are gamma, digamma, beta, log-gamma?** These are the canonical "special functions" of statistics (gamma for Gamma/Poisson normalization, digamma appears in Gamma regression and Dirichlet gradients, log-gamma for numerical stability in every GLM likelihood). Their ABSENCE from this appendix is itself a gap that should be acknowledged or filled.

## Missing examples and intuition

- **No picture of the error function.** A plot of $\operatorname{erf}$, $\operatorname{erfc}$, and $\Phi$ overlaid would instantly convey "same shape, rescaled." None is provided.
- **No picture of Owen's T wedge.** The wedge $\{X>h, 0<Y<aX\}$ (line 298) is geometric and crying out for a diagram. A noob cannot visualize it from the inequality alone.
- **No picture of the probit-vs-logistic approximation.** Lines 542-585 compare $\operatorname{expit}$ and $\Phi(\kappa z)$. A plot showing the two curves and the residual error in the "shoulders" (line 577) would make the $0.0177$ vs $0.0095$ vs $0.0040$ error numbers tangible.
- **The worked logit example (lines 631-649) is good but isolated.** It is the only place I see the approximation in action. A second example on the probit side (the existing @exm-sf-owen at lines 502-516 is fine) and one on the log-link side beyond the MGF example would balance the appendix.
- **No numerical intuition for the Sobol' advantage.** The seven-point example (lines 788-800) is nice but tiny; I never see the $O((\log N)^d/N)$ vs $O(N^{-1/2})$ gap on a realistic $N$ (say $N=1024$) where the difference is dramatic.
- **No intuition for "asymptotic vs convergent" (lines 248-251).** A beginner needs: "a convergent series gets more accurate the more terms you add forever; an asymptotic series gets more accurate up to a point and then gets worse, so you stop at the smallest term."

## Notation and jargon problems

- **$M_X(\mathbf{t})=\mathbb{E}[e^{\mathbf{t}^\top X}]$ (line 22) vs the multivariate statement $M_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{t})$ (line 47).** The notation file (notation.qmd line 77) uses $X$ not $\mathbf{X}$ in the MGF row, which is slightly inconsistent with the bold-$\mathbf{X}$ usage here. Minor, but it tripped me.
- **"$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\succeq 0$" (line 44).** The notation file defines this (line 42) as positive semidefinite, but a beginner encountering it mid-equation may not remember. A one-word parenthetical "(positive semidefinite)" on first use would help.
- **"Mills ratio" (line 229) is introduced without definition.** It is named and then a formula is given, but I do not learn that the Mills ratio is $\frac{1-\Phi(x)}{\phi(x)}$ until I reverse-engineer it from the displayed expression.
- **"the radical inverse" / "radix point" (lines 654-660).** "Radix point" is jargon; "decimal point, but in base $b$" is the plain-English gloss a noob needs.
- **"$(0,m,1)$-net" and "$(t,m,s)$-net" (lines 680, 746).** Heavy QMC jargon used as if familiar. The notation file does not define these.
- **"$\mathbb{F}_2$" (line 658 onward).** The field with two elements is named but never defined for a beginner who may not have taken abstract algebra.
- **"direction numbers" / "primitive polynomial" (lines 736-737).** These are core to Sobol' construction and are referenced (`@eq-qmc-sobol-point`) but never explained here. A noob cannot evaluate whether the construction is sound.
- **"expit" (line 21).** Defined as $e^z/(1+e^z)$, good, but the name "expit" is not universal; some books call it the logistic sigmoid or inverse logit. A one-line synonym note would orient readers from other textbooks.

## Pacing issues

- **The four sections are wildly unequal in difficulty.** Section 1 (MGF) is gentle. Section 2 (erf) is moderate. Section 3 (Owen's T) is extremely hard and contains the Plackett PDE plus a long differentiate-in-$\rho$ proof. Section 4 (Sobol') is moderate but jargon-heavy. A noob hits the wall at section 3 with no warning.
- **The intro (lines 5-27) front-loads every cross-reference at once.** I am told about `@thm-log-link-mgf`, `@thm-hypercube-integration`, `@thm-logit-no-closed-form`, `@def-mgf`, `@thm-dominated-convergence` before I have seen a single function defined. This is a dense paragraph for a beginner; consider deferring the cross-references to each section's opener.
- **Proofs precede intuition throughout.** In the Owen's T section the definition comes first, then structural facts, then the lemma, then the proof, then the probabilistic meaning. Reversing to "meaning first, then formal definition, then proof" would serve a noob far better.
- **The Notes and references section (lines 803-844) is excellent but comes too late.** It contains exactly the chapter map and historical motivation I wanted at the start. Consider promoting a condensed version to the intro.
- **The two-probit section (lines 591-649) introduces the scale-mixture idea, then says it is NOT used, then does Taylor matching instead.** This narrative detour confused me; I could not tell what was load-bearing.

## What worked well

- **The worked numerical examples are the best part.** @exm-sf-log-link (lines 135-155) walks through every arithmetic step of the log-link aggregate; @exm-sf-owen (lines 502-516) does the same for the probit second moment; @exm-sf-probit-logit (lines 631-649) shows the approximation error concretely. These are exactly what a beginner needs and they are done well.
- **The closing remark on why software standardizes on erf (lines 258-268) is clear and useful.** It explains the practical "so what" that the preceding proofs omitted.
- **The Notes section (lines 803-844) is well sourced** with explicit citations (Abramowitz and Stegun, Owen 1956, Plackett 1954, Page 1977, Monahan and Stefanski 1992, Niederreiter 1992, Sobol' 1967, Joe and Kuo 2008). As a literature map it is valuable.
- **The honesty about approximation vs identity (lines 582-585).** The remark explicitly says "the substitution is an approximation, not an identity; ML-NMR fits use exact numerical integration, and the probit form serves as a fast initializer and analytic check." This prevents a noob from over-trusting the closed form.
- **The seven-point van der Corput example (lines 788-800) is concrete and well paced.** It shows the digit reflection, the resulting set, the discrepancy, and the contrast with random points all in one short block.
- **The proofs are genuinely complete.** Nothing is left as "it can be shown"; every step is written out. For a reader who can follow the algebra, this is a trustworthy reference.
- **The cross-referencing to catalogue identifiers (e.g., "catalogue H.6" at line 807) is consistent** with the book's conventions and helps the reader who keeps the catalogue open.

## Suggestions

1. **Either rename the appendix or expand its scope.** Either retitle it "Special Functions for Numerical Integration in ML-NMR" (honest about scope), or add the missing catalog: gamma, digamma, beta, log-gamma (with the recurrence $\psi(x+1)=\psi(x)+1/x$ and the log-likelihood roles), softmax, and a proper expit/logit section. As it stands the title and the contents do not match the brief's promise.
2. **Add a "where each function is used" table at the top**, columns: function, definition line, used in chapter, used for what. The Notes section has this information implicitly; promote it.
3. **Lead every section with intuition, not definition.** For Owen's T: show the wedge picture and state "this is the probability a random point lands in a wedge" BEFORE the integral. For erf: show the curve and the relation to $\Phi$ BEFORE the series.
4. **Add diagrams.** At minimum: erf/erfc/Phi overlay; Owen's T wedge; expit vs one-probit vs two-probit with residual; Sobol' vs random points in 2D.
5. **Expand the MGF motivation to one paragraph** explaining moment-generation AND the log-link connection explicitly, so a noob sees both the general definition and the specific use.
6. **Spell out "complete the square" once** with the full expansion, then refer back to it.
7. **Add a one-paragraph interlude on asymptotic vs convergent series** before or inside @prp-erf-expansions.
8. **Add a domain connection paragraph per section:** "you will meet this when fitting a probit-link NMA to binary responder data in Chapter 21," etc. The single HTA reference at line 647 is not enough.
9. **Reorder section 3 (Owen's T) as: wedge picture → probabilistic meaning → definition → structural facts → Plackett PDE → identity.** Currently it is definition → facts → lemma → proof → meaning, which inverts the pedagogy.
10. **Define QMC jargon on first use or point to a glossary:** $(t,m,s)$-net, star discrepancy, $\mathbb{F}_2$, direction numbers, primitive polynomial, radical inverse, radix point. Either inline parentheticals or a small glossary box at the start of section 4.
11. **Acknowledge the missing functions explicitly** if they are not in scope: a one-line note "gamma, digamma, and beta functions arise in Part V likelihoods and are treated in Appendix [X]" would prevent the reader from searching this appendix for them in vain.
12. **Soften the algebra in the differentiate-in-$\rho$ proof** with intermediate checkpoints ("note that $1+a^2 = 2/(1+\rho)$, which is why the substitution works") so a noob can verify they are still following.

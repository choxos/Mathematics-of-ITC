# Feedback: Chapter 11 — Effect Modification and Collapsibility

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `chapters/part2/11-effect-modification-collapsibility.qmd`

## Overall impression

I came in believing effect modification and collapsibility were two epidemiology terms I could hand-wave through. By line 820 I had a vague sense that "odds ratios are weird and population-dependent," but I cannot honestly say I *understand* the proofs. The chapter is dense, formal, and moves at the pace of someone who already finds Jensen's inequality, Radon-Nikodym derivatives, and survival functions second nature. The conceptual framing in the intro (@sec-emcoll-intro, lines 5-51) is actually the clearest part and gave me real hope; then @sec-emcoll-cond-marg immediately plunged me into link functions, expectations over covariate densities, and a "gap lemma" with a proof that invokes Jensen in its strict form without reminding me what that is. The worked example (@exm-emcoll-worked, lines 745-815) is excellent and the one place I felt I could follow every number; it should be much earlier and much more heavily referenced. As a health researcher I kept waiting for a sentence saying "this is why your systematic review might give a different answer than another systematic review even when both are correct," and it never quite came in concrete terms.

## What was unclear

- **@sec-emcoll-intro, lines 16-21 (non-collapsibility paragraph).** "The reporting scale does not commute with averaging" is the key sentence of the whole chapter and it is dropped in without any unpacking. I do not know what "commute with averaging" means as an operation. A one-line gloss like "averaging then transforming is not the same as transforming then averaging" would have saved me re-reading this five times.
- **@def-conditional-effect / @def-marginal-effect (lines 63-90).** The two definitions are given back to back in pure formal notation. The crucial intuition, "transform-then-average versus average-then-transform," is buried at lines 92-93 *after* both definitions. I needed that intuition *before* the formulas, otherwise I am staring at two integrals that look almost identical and cannot see the order-of-operations difference.
- **@lem-emcoll-gap (lines 104-130).** "The whole reason the chapter exists" (line 101), and yet the proof is six lines. The step "we used linearity of expectation on the two conditional-effect terms" (line 124) is opaque to me; I needed to see the expansion. The jump from @eq-emcoll-delta-marg minus @eq-emcoll-delta-cond to the grouped form is a place where a beginner needs the algebra written out term by term.
- **Jensen's inequality strict form (line 127).** I have a vague memory of Jensen from a class years ago. The proof relies on it but never states it. There is a cross-reference `@thm-jensen` but no one-line reminder of what it says. For a chapter that leans so hard on convexity I expected at least a sidebar: "Jensen: for convex $g$, $g(\mathbb E[Z])\le \mathbb E[g(Z)]$, strict if $g$ strictly convex and $Z$ non-degenerate."
- **@def-effect-modifier (lines 145-155) and the three-parameter dependence (lines 157-164).** The claim that effect-modifier status "depends on the population $\mathcal P$, through the conditional mean functions, since $\mu_t^{\mathcal P}$ is itself population specific" is asserted but not illustrated. I cannot picture how the *same* covariate stops being an effect modifier just because I move to a different population. A concrete two-population example here would have made the abstract statement click.
- **@lem-emcoll-interaction (lines 177-196).** The proof says "the intercept and the prognostic main effect $\boldsymbol\beta_1^\top x$ cancel" (lines 191-192). For someone weak on linear algebra this cancellation is not obvious; I wanted to see it written. Also $\boldsymbol\beta_2^\top x$ being a dot product is not flagged, and the move from "affine function of $x$ is non-constant" to "exactly when $\beta_{2,j}\neq 0$" skips the reasoning that the other coordinates are held fixed.
- **@prp-emcoll-em-implies-pv proof (lines 309-314).** The contrapositive is clean but very terse. "Applying $g$ and subtracting" hides the fact that $g$ must be applied to *both* $\mu_a$ and $\mu_b$ at *both* $x$ and $x'$, four evaluations, before the subtraction. I had to write it out myself to follow.
- **@thm-emcoll-em-pv-distinct Part 1 proof (lines 336-346).** This is the workhorse of the whole chapter and the proof is confusingly worded. "with a common additive treatment shift folded into a relabeling, or simply take two distinct values" (line 339) is not a mathematical instruction I can execute; it reads like the author was unsure which construction to commit to. The Coq formalization with the unmeasured covariate $w$ is mentioned in the same paragraph as the pencil construction, and I lost track of which example I was supposed to be checking.
- **@lem-emcoll-anchored-cancellation (lines 359-391).** The whole lemma is the formal basis of the anchored-versus-unanchored divide, the single most important practical distinction in ITC, and its proof is a four-line integral manipulation. The notation $f(x,w)=f(w\mid x)f_X(x)$ is introduced mid-proof (line 379) without being set up. The phrase "the inner integral of the conditional density is one" (line 386) is true but I needed it stated as "because $f(w\mid x)$ is a conditional density, $\int f(w\mid x)\,dw=1$."
- **@thm-noncollapsibility-or-hr(a) proof (lines 543-605).** This is the hardest proof in the chapter and it is presented as one continuous argument with no staging. The introduction of $A(a)$ (line 553), the tilted measures $Q, Q_\omega$ (lines 564-568), the likelihood ratio $\ell$ (line 584), and the covariance identity (lines 590-592) each deserve their own labeled step. As written I cannot tell where I am in the argument. "Radon-Nikodym derivatives" (line 564) is jargon I do not have; the sentence "the weights are positive and integrate to one" (line 570) is the actual content and should lead.
- **@thm-noncollapsibility-or-hr(b) proof (lines 607-631).** The marginal hazard $\bar h_t(u)=-\bar S_t'(u)/\bar S_t(u)$ (line 616) is stated without derivation. Where does the minus sign come from? Why is the hazard the derivative of the log survival? I know this is survival analysis but the chapter assumes I remember it cold.
- **@cor-emcoll-or-attenuation (lines 634-651).** "If $W$ is degenerate at a single value $w_0$, then $X$ has one stratum" (lines 647-648): I had to pause to see why degenerate $W$ means one stratum. This deserves a half sentence.

## What wasn't elaborated enough

- **The link function $g$ (line 57 and @def-link-function).** The chapter is *about* what happens when you change $g$, so $g$ deserves more than a parenthetical. I needed a small table: identity -> risk difference, log -> risk ratio, logit -> odds ratio, log-hazard -> hazard ratio, with the *outcome range* each is defined on (probability for logit/log, positive rate for log, time for hazard). Without that, "strictly increasing, twice differentiable link on the range of the mean outcome" (lines 56-58) is abstract to the point of paralysis.
- **@eq-emcoll-nocon (lines 45-49) and the no-confounding identity.** This is invoked "freely" (line 49) but I am not confident I know what it says versus the g-formula of @thm-g-computation-unbiased. Are they the same statement? The text says one "holds by" the other but I cannot tell if it is a restatement or a consequence.
- **The four-cell table (lines 293-296).** This is the conceptual centerpiece of the EM/PV distinction and it is a 2x2 markdown table with no row of concrete numbers under it. Each cell should carry a one-line numerical instance, even if recycled from later. As it stands the upper-right "impossible" cell is justified in a parenthetical ("a varying contrast forces some arm to vary") that I found cryptic on first read.
- **Direct collapsibility vs collapsibility (lines 414-435).** The distinction is the reason the risk ratio gets its own proposition, and the verbal definitions are correct, but I never got a clean intuition for *why* the risk ratio is collapsible but not directly collapsible. The proof of @prp-emcoll-rr shows it numerically but does not explain the mechanism in words. A sentence like "collapsibility asks 'does the constant survive averaging?' while direct collapsibility asks 'does a varying effect average correctly?'" would have anchored me.
- **The remark "Non-collapsibility is not confounding" (lines 661-676).** This is the single most important conceptual message for a health researcher and it is relegated to a remark. It should be a theorem-level callout, possibly in the intro. The remark itself is well written but it comes after 600 lines of machinery, by which point a confused reader has already conflated the two.
- **@prp-emcoll-orthogonal proof (lines 718-727).** The four combinations are the chapter's payoff and each gets one sentence. Combination 3 says "the conditional odds ratio is constant at $6$ and the marginal odds ratio is $4.5$" without showing the arithmetic; I had to flip back to @exm-emcoll-worked to find it.

## Missing motivation / "why does this matter?"

- **The intro promises ITC relevance (lines 25-28) but the body delivers almost none.** Lines 25-28 say every later method "rests on knowing precisely which covariates must be balanced, on which scale." Great. Then the next 600 lines are pure measure theory with almost no ITC vocabulary. I wanted periodic "why this matters" callouts tied to Bucher, MAIC, STC, NMA. The chapter mentions @thm-bucher-unbiased (line 26, 401), @thm-sema-insufficient (line 269), @thm-failure-of-bucher (line 736), but only in passing and never with a worked ITC consequence until Step 3 of the final example (lines 788-814). That is too late.
- **@sec-emcoll-pv (lines 275-404).** This section is explicitly "the algebraic engine behind the anchored-versus-unanchored divide" (per the intro, line 35), yet the ITC meaning of anchored vs unanchored is not stated until lines 393-404, at the very end. I read 120 lines of pure algebra before learning why I should care. The slogan "prognostic variables cancel in anchored comparisons but not in unanchored ones" (line 393) is exactly the motivation I needed and it should open the section.
- **Why prove @thm-noncollapsibility-or-hr(a) at all?** The theorem is hard-won (60 lines of change-of-measure) and the practical corollary, "marginal ORs are population-specific and non-transportable," is stated in one sentence at lines 510-512 and again at lines 806-814. For a health researcher the *consequence* is the point; the proof is supporting. The chapter inverts this ratio. A short "what this buys us" paragraph right after the theorem, in ITC terms (e.g. "this is why you cannot pool marginal ORs across trials with different baseline risks, even in a perfect RCT"), would have made the effort feel justified.
- **The hazard ratio non-collapsibility (lines 536-631).** Why does a health researcher care that the marginal HR varies with time $u$? Because almost every oncology HTA reports an HR. That one-sentence motivation is missing. I had to supply it myself from domain knowledge, which a true beginner would not have.
- **@lem-emcoll-anchored-cancellation and the SEMA/SPFA acronyms.** The notation file lists SEMA (shared effect modifier assumption) and SPFA (shared prognostic factor assumption) but the chapter never names them, even though the lemma is *the* mathematical content of SEMA. A beginner reading the ITC literature will meet SEMA immediately; the chapter should bridge to that name.

## Missing examples and intuition

- **No diagram.** A figure showing "transform-then-average" vs "average-then-transform" as two paths through a commutative-diagram-style square would make @lem-emcoll-gap trivial to remember. The chapter is entirely text and equations.
- **No numeric preview of the gap before the proof.** @lem-emcoll-gap is stated abstractly. A single concrete number, e.g. "for the worked example, $J_{\mathrm{logit}}(\mu_1)-J_{\mathrm{logit}}(\mu_0)=\log 4.5-\log 6=-0.288$," placed right after the lemma, would have made the abstraction tangible before the proof.
- **@prp-emcoll-scale (lines 238-265).** Good that it is numeric, but the stratum risks $\mu_0(0)=0.1,\mu_1(0)=0.2,\mu_0(1)=0.5,\mu_1(1)=0.6$ appear with no setup. Where did these come from? Are they realistic? A one-sentence framing ("imagine a younger stratum with low baseline risk and an older stratum with high baseline risk, and a drug that adds a constant 0.1 absolute risk in both") would have made the numbers feel like data rather than arbitrary decimals.
- **No example of effect modification depending on population.** Lines 157-164 claim it does; no example demonstrates it. I wanted two populations with the same $\mu_t$ functions but different supports of $X$, showing $X$ is an EM in one and not the other, or vice versa.
- **No example of "prognostic but not EM" with a real covariate name.** Everything is $\mu_a(0),\mu_a(1)$. Calling one example "age" or "baseline severity" would have grounded the abstraction for a health reader.
- **The hazard-ratio example (lines 607-631) has no health context.** Exponential hazards $h_0(0)=1, h_0(1)=4$ with no clinical gloss. Saying "stratum 1 is a high-risk subgroup, e.g. advanced disease" would cost nothing and help a lot.

## Notation and jargon problems

- **$\tau^g_{ab}(x)$ vs $\psi_g(x)$.** Lines 75-76 introduce $\psi_g(x)=\tau^g_{01}(x)$ "matching the notation of @def-non-collapsibility." Now I have two symbols for the same thing and I do not know which to use when. The chapter uses both freely. Pick one, or state clearly when each is conventional.
- **$\Delta^{\mathrm{Cond},g}_{ab}(\mathcal P)$ vs $\Delta^{\mathrm{Marg},g}_{ab}(\mathcal P)$ vs $\psi_g^{\mathrm M}$.** Three notational systems for the two estimands appear by line 88. The superscripts $\mathrm{Cond}$ and $\mathrm{Marg}$ are clear; the $\psi_g^{\mathrm M}$ alias is not flagged as an alias at first use in proofs, so when @prp-emcoll-rd uses $\psi_{\mathrm{id}}^{\mathrm M}$ (line 455) I had to backtrack.
- **$J_g(\mu_t)$ (line 110).** This is the Jensen defect, the central object, and it gets a one-line definition and the symbol $J_g$. Calling it the "Jensen gap" or "nonlinearity gap" in words, at least once, would help.
- **"Radon-Nikodym derivatives" (line 564) and "tilted probability measures" (line 564).** Pure jargon for a beginner. The concept is just "reweight by $1/(1+aW)$ and renormalize." The jargon should come *after* the plain-language version, not instead of it.
- **"Affine" (line 112).** The chapter assumes I know affine means linear-plus-constant. A parenthetical "(i.e. linear plus a constant, $g(u)=cu+d$)" the first time would cost one line.
- **$\operatorname{Cov}_Q$ (line 591) and the covariance identity.** Writing $\mathbb E_{Q_\omega}[W]-\mathbb E_Q[W]=\operatorname{Cov}_Q(W,\ell(W))$ (lines 590-592) uses $\mathbb E_Q[\ell(W)]=1$ from the previous line. The identity $\mathbb E[XY]-\mathbb E[X]\mathbb E[Y]=\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ is used silently; flag it.
- **The "correlation inequality for oppositely monotone functions" (lines 593-596).** Named as if it is a standard result; it is not in the notation file and not cross-referenced. State it as a lemma or at least give the one-line intuition "if one function goes up while the other goes down, their covariance is non-positive."
- **$\blacktriangleleft$ and $\square$.** Defined in notation.qmd (line 21) but a beginner reading only this chapter will not know. A footnote on first use would help.
- **$f_{\mathcal P}$, $f_X$, $f(w\mid x)$.** The chapter switches between $f_{\mathcal P}$ (population density), $f_X$ (marginal of $X$), and $f(w\mid x)$ without a notation roundup. Line 379 piles all three into one equation.
- **"$\mu_t^{\mathcal P}(x)$" vs "$\mu_t(x)$".** The superscript $\mathcal P$ is sometimes present (lines 59-60, 283) and sometimes dropped (line 73 onward). I was never sure whether dropping it meant "fixed population" or "same for all populations."

## Pacing issues

- **@sec-emcoll-cond-marg (lines 53-139) is too fast.** It introduces conditional effect, marginal effect, the gap lemma, and the affine/convex/concave trichotomy in 87 lines. This is the conceptual foundation of the chapter and it deserves roughly double the space, with the worked example previewed here rather than 600 lines later.
- **@sec-emcoll-em-interaction (lines 141-273) is well paced** until @prp-emcoll-scale, then the methodological corollary (lines 267-273) crams SEMA, @thm-sema-insufficient, scale alignment, and the shadow-of-non-collapsibility idea into seven lines. That is too much for the end of a section.
- **@sec-emcoll-pv (lines 275-404) front-loads abstraction.** The four-cell table (lines 293-296), @prp-emcoll-em-implies-pv, @thm-emcoll-em-pv-distinct, and @lem-emcoll-anchored-cancellation all come before the single explanatory paragraph at lines 393-404. Reverse this order: motivate, then prove.
- **@sec-emcoll-collapse (lines 406-688) is the heaviest section and the longest.** It contains four definitions/propositions and the two big non-collapsibility theorems, totaling ~280 lines with no internal breathing room. Subsection breaks (already present for the OR/HR non-collapsibility, line 506) would help inside the odds-ratio proof itself.
- **The odds-ratio proof (lines 543-605) is one block.** It should be broken into labeled steps (setup, reweighting, covariance comparison, bounds), each with a one-line "what we just showed" sentence.
- **The worked example (@exm-emcoll-worked, lines 745-815) comes too late.** It is the pedagogical anchor of the entire chapter and it sits after the synthesis section. A reader who is lost by line 200 will never reach it. At minimum, a forward pointer at line 100 ("see @exm-emcoll-worked for a fully numeric instance of everything below") would have kept me going.
- **Exercises (lines 868-928).** Several are starred ★ and reference machinery (saturated logistic model, frailty selection) not developed in the chapter body. For a beginner these read as oracles. @exr-emcoll-or-attenuation asks me to complete the proof for continuous $W$ but the chapter proof already claims to handle the general case (line 604 "identical with inequalities reversed"), so I am confused about what is left to do.

The sentence "the per-patient story is perfectly homogeneous, and the disagreement is an arithmetic artifact of the chosen effect measure" (lines 18-19) is the best single sentence in the chapter.
- **The roadmap (lines 30-39).** Clear, section-by-section. I always knew where I was in the plan.
- **@prp-emcoll-scale (lines 238-265).** The numeric proof is the first place I fully followed the argument. The risk-difference-constant / logit-varying contrast is the perfect illustration.
- **The "Three differences" list (lines 211-230): symmetry, scale dependence, manipulability.** Crisp, well separated, and each tied to a citation. This is the kind of structured contrast that helps a beginner.
- **The remark "Non-collapsibility is not confounding" (lines 661-676).** Once I reached it, it was the clearest statement of the chapter's conceptual core. Well written; it just needs to be earlier and more prominent.
- **The summary table (lines 683-688).** Compact and exactly what I wanted. I copied it into my notes.
- **@exm-emcoll-worked Step 1-3 (lines 745-815).** Three scales, marginal effects, ITC consequence, all by hand. This is a model for how to teach this material. If the whole chapter were paced like this it would be outstanding.
- **@prp-emcoll-orthogonal (lines 702-727).** The four-combinations framing is exactly the synthesis I needed, and the proof maps each combination to a concrete earlier result.
- **The notes and references (@sec-emcoll-notes, lines 824-866).** Honest about what is classical, what is from Chandler-Ishak, what is machine-checked. Useful for a beginner who wants to read further.

4. **Break the odds-ratio non-collapsibility proof into four labeled steps** with a "what we showed" sentence after each, and rewrite the Radon-Nikodym sentence in plain reweighting language first.
5. **Add a small link-function table** (identity/log/logit/log-hazard -> measure -> outcome range) at first mention of $g$ in @sec-emcoll-cond-marg.
6. **Promote the "Non-collapsibility is not confounding" remark to a theorem or a boxed callout** in @sec-emcoll-intro or right after @lem-emcoll-gap, not buried at line 661.
7. **Name SEMA and SPFA explicitly** where the corresponding assumptions first appear (@lem-emcoll-anchored-cancellation, lines 359-391), with a forward reference to the transportability chapter.
8. **Add one diagram** (commutative square for transform-then-average vs average-then-transform) and one figure (sketch of $\overline{\mathrm{HR}}(u)$ vs $r$ over time) to break the wall of equations.
9. **Standardize the $\mu_t^{\mathcal P}(x)$ vs $\mu_t(x)$ notation** or state explicitly once that the superscript is dropped when the population is fixed.

11. **Reword the @thm-emcoll-em-pv-distinct Part 1 proof** to commit to a single construction (the pencil one or the Coq one) and drop the "or simply take two distinct values" hedge at line 339.

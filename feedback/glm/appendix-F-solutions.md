# Feedback: Appendix F — Solutions to Selected Exercises

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `appendices/F-solutions.qmd`

## Overall impression

As a beginner I could follow the fully numerical solutions (Chapters 13, 20, 26, 29) end to end, but most of the proof-style solutions lost me partway through because they skip the algebraic manipulations and the "why this trick" motivation that I need to reproduce the work myself. The biggest barrier is not the math itself but the pacing: again and again a clever construction (define `z` this way, test on `v = m + tw`, introduce `A(a) = E[aW/(1+aW)]/E[1/(1+aW)]`) appears with no sentence explaining where it came from, so I cannot see how I would ever have thought of it. The ITC/health connection is usually a single closing clause, which is too thin for someone learning the subject through these solutions.

## What was unclear

- **`@exr-la1-dim-sum` (lines 30-41).** I got lost in the independence argument. You write "Set z = Σ_s γ_s b_s" and then deduce z ∈ U from the displayed equation, but you never say *why* I should define z that way. I tried to follow and could not see the motivation for isolating the b-sum before looking at the a-sum. A one-line "we isolate the W-only part so we can use W-basis independence" would have saved me.
- **`@exr-oblique-vs-orthogonal` (lines 133-141).** The (iii)⇒(i) step tests the contraction inequality on `v = m + tw` and then divides by `t > 0` and `t < 0` separately to force ⟨m,w⟩ = 0. I do not understand why `v = m + tw` is the right test vector, and I do not understand the "let t↓0" / "t↑0" squeeze. This is a standard trick but it is presented as if obvious.
- **`@exr-weyl-inequality` (lines 171-203).** The Courant-Fischer min-max form is stated but not motivated; I do not know why `λ_k(M) = min_{dim S=k} max R_M(x)`. The Rayleigh quotient additivity `R_{A+B} = R_A + R_B` is used as if obvious. The lower-bound trick "apply the upper bound to the pair A+B and -B" is slick and I would never have found it; one sentence on the symmetry motivation would help.
- **`@exr-cov-psd-construction` (line 231).** You write `Var(X1 - X2) = q_-^T Σ q_- · 2 = 0`. Where does the factor `· 2` come from? `X1 - X2 = (1,-1)·(X1,X2) = √2 · q_-^T X`, so `Var = 2 · q_-^T Σ q_-`. I had to derive this myself; the factor 2 appears from nowhere and I initially thought it was a typo.
- **`@exr-graphoid-counterexample` (lines 238-264).** The graphoid intersection axiom (G5) is referenced via `@thm-graphoid-axioms` but not restated, so I cannot check the counterexample against the axiom without flipping chapters. "Positivity" is used throughout (lines 253, 259, 263) but never defined in this solution; as a noob I am not sure whether positivity means "p(x) > 0 for all x" or something stronger.
- **`@exr-information-equality-fail` (lines 274-278).** "Differentiating ∫₀^θ θ⁻¹ dx = 1 picks up a boundary term that the naive score omits." I know roughly what you mean but I cannot reproduce the boundary term. You need to actually show the Leibniz step: `d/dθ ∫₀^θ θ⁻¹ dx = θ⁻¹·1 + ∫₀^θ ∂_θ(θ⁻¹) dx`, with the first term being the boundary contribution the naive score misses. Right now this is stated, not derived.
- **`@exr-sandwich-derivation` (lines 318-320).** `B(μ₀) = Var(X)/μ₀² = v₀/μ₀² = κμ₀/μ₀² = κ/μ₀`. Where was `κ` defined? It appears at line 319 with no introduction. I infer `κ = v₀/μ₀` is the overdispersion factor (variance-to-mean ratio), but this should be stated before it is used. The same `κ` drives the whole punchline in (c), so a noob who misses it misses everything.
- **`@exr-glm-irls-probit` (lines 460-468).** The observed-versus-expected information paragraph is the most opaque passage in the appendix. You say the product rule produces "a term proportional to the data residual (y_i - μ_i) times d/dη_i of the factor (dμ_i/dη_i)/Var(Y_i)" but you never actually write the derivative. I cannot tell whether the coefficient `d/dη [φ(η)/(Φ(η)(1-Φ(η)))]` vanishes or not without computing it, and you assert it "does not vanish" without showing it. For a noob this reads as a claim, not a derivation.
- **`@exr-james-stein-sure` (lines 548-555).** The divergence calculation is too compressed. `∂g_j/∂y_j = -(J-2)σ² (‖y‖² - 2y_j²)/‖y‖⁴` — I could not reproduce this; the derivative of `y_j/‖y‖²` needs the product/quotient rule and you skip it. Then `∇·g = -(J-2)σ² (J‖y‖² - 2‖y‖²)/‖y‖⁴` uses `Σ_j y_j² = ‖y‖²` to combine, but that step is hidden. Please expand both into two intermediate lines.
- **`@exr-reml-one-way` (lines 591-601).** "Two standard facts make the balanced computation explicit" and then two chi-square distributional results are stated. I am asked to trust that `I(n-1)MS_W/σ² ~ χ²_{I(n-1)}` and `(I-1)MS_B/(nτ²+σ²) ~ χ²_{I-1}` with no derivation. "Error contrasts orthogonal to 1_{In}" is jargon I do not have from Chapter 8's solution. This solution reads as a summary for someone who already knows ANOVA.
- **`@exr-limits-general-attenuation` (lines 1167-1206).** The second-order Taylor expansion, the "apply g and expand again using g'(ψ(m)) = 1/ψ'(m)" step, and the `E[Φ(a+ρX)] = Φ((a+ρx̄)/√(1+ρ²σ²))` identity are all stated without derivation. The last one is a standard Gaussian integral identity but a noob cannot verify it; I would like to see the squaring-and-completing-the-square derivation, or at least a pointer to where it is proved in Chapter 4.
- **`@exr-maic-sandwich-vs-naive` (lines 1342-1382).** This is the hardest solution in the appendix for me. The block lower-triangular Jacobian, the claim "the influence function of μ̂_A is the second row of -A⁻¹ψ", and the final variance expression `Var(ψ₂ - (a₂₁/a₁₁)ψ₁)/(n a₂₂²)` all appear without enough intermediate steps. I do not know why the influence function is the second row of that product; a noob needs the chain from `√n(θ̂-θ) = -A⁻¹ · (1/√n)Σψ` down to the row extraction.
- **`@exr-stc-loglink-cancellation` (lines 1398-1402).** `½β_(A)^T Σ β_(A) - ½β_(C)^T Σ β_(C) = β₁^T Σ β₂ + ½β₂^T Σ β₂`. I could not expand this without writing it out; please show `(β₁+β₂)^T Σ (β₁+β₂) - β₁^T Σ β₁ = 2β₁^T Σ β₂ + β₂^T Σ β₂` as an intermediate line. The cancellation conclusion rests on exactly this algebra.
- **`@exr-qmc-logit-pipeline` (line 1663).** `Var(η) = Var(X₁ + 0.5X₂) = 1.25 + ρ`. I get `1 + 0.25 = 1.25` for the variance terms but the cross term `2·0.5·Cov(X₁,X₂) = ρ` is not shown; a noob needs the `Var(aX+bY) = a²VarX + b²VarY + 2ab·Cov` line made explicit.
- **`@exr-comp-gpd` (lines 1770-1775).** "As x→∞, f(x) ~ C x^{-1/k-1}". I could see the density formula but not why this asymptotic holds; one line of "factor out the dominant power" manipulation would make the tail-integral convergence check reproducible.
- **`@exr-genlik-mhr-attenuation` (line 1816).** `g'(c) = -Var_c(ρ)`. This is the key identity and it is stated, not derived. I need to see `g'(c) = d/dc [E[ρ e^{-ρc}]/E[e^{-ρc}]]` worked out with the quotient rule to land on `-Var_c(ρ)`. Without it the whole monotonicity argument is a black box.
- **`@exr-qba-itc-evalue-star` (lines 2042-2045).** The constant `K = β₀ + β_R m_R + ½β_R²σ_R² + ½β_U²σ_U² - log μ₀` appears with no derivation. I do not know where this formula comes from, and since the entire sensitivity curve and E-value flow from `K`, a noob who cannot recompute `K` is sunk. Please add a sentence deriving it from the log-linear model, or at least cite the exact equation in Chapter 28.

- **`@exr-po-positivity-failure` (lines 654-659).** The ITC overlap link is a single clause. - **`@exr-glm-irls-probit` (lines 437-469).** The solution front-loads the working weights and working response (fine) and then crams the observed-versus-expected information contrast into a single dense paragraph at the end (lines 457-468). ## Suggestions

1. Add a "Notation and prerequisites" paragraph at the top of each solution that re-states the one or two key results it leans on (e.g., for `@exr-weyl-inequality`: "Recall from `@thm-courant-fischer` that `λ_k(M) = min_{dim S=k} max R_M(x)`; the min-max form is the only fact we need."). Right now too many solutions assume I have the chapter open beside me.
2. For every proof with a clever construction (define `z`, test `v = m+tw`, introduce `A(a)`, apply the upper bound to `A+B` and `-B`), insert one *motivation* sentence before the construction: "The trick is to ... so that we can use ...". This is the single change that would most help a noob.
3. Expand the compressed algebra in the flagged passages: the `@exr-james-stein-sure` divergence, the `@exr-lr-general-hypothesis` quadratic expansion, the `@exr-stc-loglink-cancellation` slope algebra, the `@exr-transport-attenuation` `φ''` computation, and the `@exr-genlik-mhr-attenuation` `g'(c) = -Var_c(ρ)` identity. Each needs two or three intermediate display lines.
4. Define `κ` (overdispersion ratio) in `@exr-sandwich-derivation` before using it, and define "working" log-likelihood in the same solution. These two fixes alone unlock the whole Chapter 5 solution for a beginner.
5. End every abstract proof with a small numerical sanity check of the style used in `@exr-transport-attenuation` and `@exr-agg-prevalence`: pick the chapter's running example, plug in, show the bound/identity holds. This is already the appendix's best habit; it should be universal.
6. Split the multi-implication solutions (`@exr-oblique-vs-orthogonal`, `@exr-limits-general-attenuation`, `@exr-maic-sandwich-vs-naive`) into labeled sub-parts (a)/(b)/(c) matching the structure of `@exr-glm-noncollapse` and `@exr-ma-fe-pipeline`. Walls of implications are the hardest format for a weak-math reader.
7. Expand each closing ITC tie-in from one dependent clause to two or three sentences that name the concrete method (MAIC, STC, ML-NMR, Bucher) and the concrete consequence for a health technology assessment: what goes wrong in a drug comparison if this result is ignored. The current one-liners are too thin for someone learning the subject through the solutions.
8. Re-derive or explicitly cross-reference the load-bearing cited identities that currently appear from nowhere: the `E[Φ(a+ρX)]` Gaussian identity in `@exr-limits-general-attenuation`, the lognormal Pearson correlation `(e^{ρσ²}-1)/(e^{σ²}-1)` in `@exr-qba-norta-margins-star`, the `K` constant in `@exr-qba-itc-evalue-star`, and the `g'(c) = -Var_c(ρ)` identity in `@exr-genlik-mhr-attenuation`. For each, either show the derivation or point to the exact equation number where it is proved.
9. Add a glossary margin-note or parenthetical for specialized jargon on first use in a solution: "working" (M-estimation), "error contrast" (REML), "Hajek-form" (IPW), "risk-set weighted" (survival), "Frechet bounds / comonotone" (copulas), "PSIS-LOO" (Bayesian computation). A noob reading the solutions in order meets all of these cold.
10. Verify completeness: the preface says every starred exercise has a solution here, but as a reader I have no way to confirm that without scanning all thirty chapters. A one-line "starred exercises covered: N" count per chapter, or a checklist at the head of the appendix, would let me confirm I am not missing a solution I expected.

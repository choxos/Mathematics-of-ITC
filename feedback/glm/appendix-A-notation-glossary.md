# Feedback: Appendix A — Notation Glossary

**Reviewer perspective:** Beginner health researcher, basic calculus, weak math foundation.
**File:** `appendices/A-notation-glossary.qmd`

## Overall impression

I came to this appendix hoping it would be the one place I could look up a symbol I saw in a chapter and immediately understand it. It is a handsome, well-organized pair of tables, and I respect the ambition of collecting every symbol and every defined term in one spot. But as a confused reader with weak math, I kept hitting walls. The symbol tables are too terse to actually teach me anything; they tell me a slug to chase but give me almost no help in the moment. Worse, large families of symbols that I see all over Chapters 8 and 24 (the Bayesian and computation chapters) are simply absent from both the tables here and from `notation.qmd` itself. The alphabetical term glossary is more useful, but even there the one-sentence meanings lean on jargon that a beginner has not yet met. The appendix advertises itself as a "single place to look up a symbol" (line 15), and for the symbols it lists it mostly delivers, but it is not complete and it is not self-contained enough for a noob.

## What was unclear

- **$A\succeq 0,\ A\succ 0$ (line 44).** I see the gloss "positive semidefinite; positive definite" and I have no idea what that means. I am sent to `@def-definiteness` in Ch. 3, which is fine, but the whole point of a glossary is to give me a fighting chance before I click away. A parenthetical like "(the matrix's quadratic form is always $\ge 0$, or strictly $>0$ away from zero)" would have let me keep reading.
- **$P_{\mathcal{M}},\ P^{\perp}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (line 43).** "orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{M}$ and its complement." For a noob, "projection" and "orthogonal complement" are themselves undefined here. I looked this up and couldn't find a plain-language hint that this is "the nearest point in the subspace" and "everything left over."
- **$\lambda_i(A),\ \sigma_i(A)$ (line 45).** "i-th eigenvalue; i-th singular value." Eigenvalue and singular value are two of the most opaque words in the book for a beginner, and the glossary offers zero intuition. I had to go to Ch. 3 just to learn whether these are the same thing.
- **$X\perp Y\mid Z$ (line 73).** The gloss says "conditional independence" and links `@def-conditional-independence`. But the symbol $\perp$ itself is never explained as "is independent of." I read it as "perpendicular" because that is what $\perp$ meant in the linear algebra table two sections earlier (line 293, "orthogonality ... whose inner product is zero"). Using the same glyph for two unrelated ideas, with no note, genuinely confused me.
- **$\mathcal{C}(A),\ \mathcal{N}(A)$ (line 40).** "column space; null space." Calligraphic C and N with no gloss of what those spaces *are* in one line. I had to chase `@def-column-space` and `@def-kernel-image` to learn that null space means "everything A sends to zero."
- **$M_X(\mathbf{t})=\mathbb{E}[e^{\mathbf{t}^\top X}]$ (line 79).** "moment generating function." I do not know what a moment generating function *does* from this row. The term glossary entry (line 286) is only slightly better ("which fixes the law when finite near the origin"). "Fixes the law" is jargon for "determines the distribution," and that paraphrase is missing here.
- **$O_p,\ o_p,\ O(\cdot),\ o(\cdot)$ (line 82).** "stochastic and deterministic order symbols." This is the single most cryptic line in the probability table. A beginner has no idea what "order symbol" means, what the difference between big-O and little-o is, or why it matters. The glossary gives me nothing.
- **$\xrightarrow{p},\ \xrightarrow{d},\ \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}$ (line 81).** Three arrows, three convergence modes, zero intuition. "almost surely" in particular is a phrase that means almost nothing to someone with basic calculus, and the gloss does not help.

## What wasn't elaborated enough

- **The entire Bayesian and computation symbol family is missing.** Chapters 8 and 24 use $\Pi$ (prior measure), $\pi(\boldsymbol\xi)$ (prior density), $\pi(\boldsymbol\xi\mid\mathbf y)$ (posterior density), $\hat R$ (Gelman-Rubin diagnostic), $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ or "effective sample size of an MCMC chain," $\mathrm{lppd}$, $\widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}_{\mathrm{loo}}$, $\mathrm{WAIC}$, $\mathrm{LOO}$, PSIS, the Pareto tail index $\hat k$, the deviance $D$, $p_D$, and the heterogeneity variance $\tau^2$ and $\kappa^2$. None of these appear in any symbol table in this appendix, and none appear in `notation.qmd` either. The term glossary does define some of the *concepts* (credible set, exchangeability, HMC, DIC/WAIC/LOO-CV, PSIS-LOO, the two ESS notions), but the *symbols* themselves are nowhere. I looked up $\hat R$ in Ch. 24 and could not find it here; I looked up $\pi(\boldsymbol\xi\mid\mathbf y)$ and could not find it here. This is the biggest gap in the appendix.
- **Two different "ESS" meanings collide without enough warning.** The symbol table defines $\mathrm{ESS}=(\sum_i w_i)^2/\sum_i w_i^2$ (line 151, weighting) and the term glossary defines "Effective sample size of an MCMC chain" (line 243, autocorrelation-deflated). `notation.qmd` flags the clash between $\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and the weighting ESS (lines 60 to 62 of `notation.qmd`), but this appendix never warns me that the *same three-letter abbreviation* also denotes the MCMC quantity. I read "ESS" in Ch. 24, came here, and found only the weighting formula.
- **$\mathbb{V}$ (line 94).** "sandwich (robust) variance estimator." That is all. Which sandwich, $A^{-1}BA^{-\top}$? The term glossary (line 325) does give the formula, but the symbol table should at least point me there, because a reader looking up the symbol will not necessarily think to also search the term table.
- **$S$ (line 110).** "study or population membership indicator." In Ch. 8 the same glyph $\Pi$ is used as a measure, and in Ch. 27 $\Pi(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})$ is the set of couplings for the Wasserstein distance. None of these overloaded uses of $S$ and $\Pi$ are cross-referenced. I could not tell from this row which $S$ a given chapter meant.
- **$\boldsymbol{\xi}$ (line 142).** "full parameter vector." This is the single most-used symbol in the second half of the book, and it gets one line. What is in it? In ML-NMR it bundles $\mu_j,\boldsymbol{\beta}_1,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{2,k},\gamma_k$; I had to read Ch. 20 to learn that. A parenthetical listing the components would have saved me a chapter trip.
- **$d_{ab(\mathcal{P})}$ (line 124).** "marginal relative effect of $b$ versus $a$ in $\mathcal{P}$." On what scale? The notation.qmd entry (line 116) adds "on a stated scale," but this appendix drops that qualifier, so I assumed it was scale-free and was confused when later chapters distinguished link-scale from identity-scale versions.

## Missing motivation / "why does this matter?"

Almost no entry tells me *why* a symbol is defined the way it is. A glossary that is a pure lookup table is fine for an expert, but the book's stated goal is to take a naive researcher to mastery, and motivation is part of that.

- **$\tau(x)=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)$ (line 108).** I am told it is the CATE, but not why we bother defining the conditional version before the average. The averaging step $\tau=\mathbb{E}[\tau(X)]$ (line 109) is the whole point (you can only identify $\tau$ if you can average over the covariate distribution), and that motivation is invisible here.
- **$e(x)=P(T=1\mid X=x)$ (line 106).** Why is the propensity score special? The glossary does not say it is "the coarsest balancing score" (that phrase appears only in the term glossary, line 309). A reader meeting $e(x)$ in a chapter will not know to look at the term table.
- **$H=\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top$ (line 58).** I get the formula and the label "hat matrix," but no hint that it is called the hat matrix *because* it puts the hat on $\mathbf{y}$. That one-sentence motivation would make the symbol stick.
- **$w_i=\exp(\alpha+\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf{x}_i)$ (line 152).** Why an exponential tilt? The term glossary (line 246) mentions "the solution is an exponential tilt," but the symbol table does not tell me the exponential form is forced by the balance constraints. I had to chase the entropy-balancing definition to learn that.
- **$C_\Omega$ Gaussian copula (line 155).** No motivation for *why* the Gaussian copula is the one used in ML-NMR integration. A beginner sees a scary formula and no reason it matters.

## Missing examples and intuition

- No entry in either table carries a worked mini-example. Even one concrete instance per topic group (for example, "$\mathbf{e}_1=(1,0,\dots,0)$" next to line 35, or "$e(x)=0.3$ means 30 percent of those with covariates $x$ were treated" next to line 106) would massively help a noob.
- The convergence arrows (line 81) cry out for a one-line intuition: "$\xrightarrow{p}$: the estimate settles down to a fixed value as $n$ grows."
- The order symbols (line 82) need a one-line reading guide: "$O(1/n)$ means 'shrinks like $1/n$'."
- The copula row (line 154) would benefit from a plain-language gloss: "a function that glues marginal distributions into a joint distribution."

## Notation and jargon problems

- **Undefined jargon inside glossary entries.** Several one-sentence meanings use terms a beginner has not met: "natural parameter" (line 210, canonical link), "exponential dispersion family" assumes I know what a dispersion family is, "measurable function" (line 314, random variable) assumes measure theory, "$\sigma$-algebra" (line 306) is used without paraphrase, "idempotent" (line 294) is not paraphrased, "diffeomorphic reparameterization" appears in Ch. 24 prose that the glossary points to. A true noob glossary should paraphrase each piece of jargon once.
- **Same glyph, different meaning, no disambiguation.** $\perp$ means "perpendicular" in linear algebra (line 293) and "conditionally independent of" in probability (line 73). $\Pi$ is a prior measure (Ch. 8), a set of couplings (Ch. 27), and the permutation group in the determinant formula (Ch. 3). $S$ is a study/population indicator (line 110) and also a generic set in linear algebra (line 33, $\mathrm{span}(S)$). ESS means two different things (line 151 vs line 243). The glossary should flag every one of these collisions explicitly; right now it flags only the $\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ clash (and only in `notation.qmd`, not here).
- **Inconsistency with `notation.qmd`.** The appendix says it "reproduces" `notation.qmd` (line 357), but it quietly drops detail. `notation.qmd` line 116 says $d_{ab(\mathcal{P})}$ is "on a stated scale"; the appendix (line 124) omits that. `notation.qmd` line 75 calls $F_X^{-1}$ the "generalized inverse (quantile)"; the appendix (line 77) says only "quantile inverse," which is less precise and could be read as the ordinary inverse, which is wrong for discrete distributions. `notation.qmd` line 40 gives the explicit formula $\|\mathbf{x}\|_2=\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{x}}$; the appendix (line 42) drops it. These are small losses but they accumulate for a confused reader.
- **Alphabetical glossary ordering is fragile.** Terms are sorted by "leading term ignoring case, punctuation, and parentheticals" (line 187). This puts "Collapsibility and direct collapsibility" (line 215) and "Collapsibility and non-collapsibility of an effect measure" (line 216) next to each other, which is good, but it also splits "Marginal effect" (line 280) from "Marginal STC" (line 283) and "Marginal likelihood and Bayes factor" (line 281) by unrelated entries, and it buries "STC estimands" (line 335) far from "STC outcome model" (line 336). A noob looking for everything about STC has to scan the whole table. A "see also" cross-reference or a topic index would help.
- **The "Introduced" column mixes chapters and slugs inconsistently.** Some rows give a slug, some do not, with no visible rule. $\mathbf{1}_n,\mathbf{0}_n$ (line 36) and $I_n,J_n$ (line 37) have no slug; $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ has a slug but $\det,\mathrm{tr}$ (line 39) do not. I could not tell whether the missing slugs mean "no formal definition exists" or "the author forgot."

## Pacing issues

- The jump from the linear-algebra table (foundations) to the regression table (line 52) is abrupt. A one-line bridge saying "the next group is the statistics a researcher actually sees" would orient a beginner.
- The probability table (lines 68 to 82) crams distributions, expectation, independence, convergence, and order symbols into one block. For a weak-math reader this is the densest part of the appendix, and there is no sub-grouping to help me find, say, "which row is about convergence."
- The Bayesian and computation material that *should* sit between inference (line 86) and causal inference (line 98) is entirely absent, so the reader goes straight from sandwich variance to potential outcomes with no transition for the entire Bayesian apparatus of Chapters 8 and 24.
- The term glossary is one giant 160-row table (lines 192 to 352) with no section breaks. For a noob this is a wall; grouping by part or by topic, with sub-headers, would make lookup far faster.

## What worked well

- The two-part structure (symbols by topic, then terms alphabetically) is sensible and easy to remember.
- The slugs in the "Introduced" and "Defined in" columns are exactly the cross-reference keys an author would use, so following one in the rendered book should jump me to the defining environment. That is genuinely useful.
- The note that "many symbols recur across the book; the chapter named is the one that introduces and is responsible for the symbol" (lines 25 to 26) sets the right expectation and prevents me from thinking a symbol only appears in one place.
- The abbreviations table (lines 162 to 182) is clear, complete-feeling, and gives chapter pointers; it is the most beginner-friendly table in the appendix.
- The scale-dependence caveat for effect modification and collapsibility (lines 189 to 190) is a smart callout; those terms genuinely mean different things on different links, and flagging it once up front is good.
- The "Notes and sources" section (lines 355 to 366) correctly points me to the companion appendices (C distributions, B matrix calculus, D special functions, G Coq), which is exactly the kind of signposting a lost reader needs.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a Bayesian and computation symbol table** covering $\Pi$, $\pi(\boldsymbol\xi)$, $\pi(\boldsymbol\xi\mid\mathbf y)$, $\hat R$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (MCMC), $\mathrm{lppd}$, $\widehat{\mathrm{elpd}}$, $\mathrm{WAIC}$, $\mathrm{LOO}$, PSIS, $\hat k$, $D$, $p_D$, $\tau^2$, $\kappa^2$. Mirror this in `notation.qmd`, which currently has no Bayesian section at all.
2. **Add a one-line plain-language gloss to every symbol row**, aimed at someone who has never seen the term. Keep the existing one-line meaning, append a second clause in parentheses. For example, line 44: "positive semidefinite; positive definite (the quadratic form $\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x}$ is always $\ge 0$, or strictly $>0$ for $\mathbf{x}\ne 0$)."
3. **Flag every glyph collision explicitly.** Add a short " overloaded" note wherever $\perp$, $\Pi$, $S$, ESS, $\tau$, $\mu$, $\sigma^2$ are reused with a different meaning, and point to the other rows. Right now only the $\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ clash is flagged, and only in `notation.qmd`.
4. **Restore the detail dropped from `notation.qmd`.** Re-add "on a stated scale" to $d_{ab(\mathcal{P})}$ (line 124), "generalized inverse (quantile)" to $F_X^{-1}$ (line 77), and the explicit norm formula to line 42. The appendix claims to reproduce `notation.qmd`; it should actually do so.
5. **Paraphrase jargon once per term in the alphabetical glossary.** "measurable function," "$\sigma$-algebra," "idempotent," "diffeomorphic," "natural parameter," "dispersion family" each deserve a parenthetical plain-English gloss the first time they appear.
6. **Group the term glossary by Part or by topic** with sub-headers, or at least add "see also" pointers so that all STC entries, all collapsibility entries, all population-adjustment entries sit near each other.
7. **Add a worked mini-example to at least one row per topic group.** One concrete number next to $\mathbf{e}_i$, $e(x)$, $\tau(x)$, $w_i$, $C_\Omega$ would make the appendix teach, not just list.
8. **Add a "how to use this glossary" paragraph** at the top, explaining that the symbol table is for "I saw this glyph, what is it" and the term table is for "I heard this word, what does it mean," and that following a slug jumps to the full definition. Right now that distinction is implicit.
